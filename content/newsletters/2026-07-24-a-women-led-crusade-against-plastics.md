## A Women-Led Crusade Against Plastics

When I first picked up Judith Enck’s [*The Problem with Plastic: How We Can Save Ourselves and Our Planet Before It's Too Late*,](https://bookshop.org/p/books/the-problem-with-plastic-how-we-can-save-ourselves-and-our-planet-before-it-s-too-late-adam-mahoney/bf84a2cbfebc732a?ean=9781620979457\&bkshp-astro=t) I expected a gloomy read. 

 

I had seen photos of vast expanses of [plastic pollution choking our ecosystems,](https://loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=23-P13-00015\&segmentID=3) read about the growing body of research on the [negative human health impacts of microplastics,](https://loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=23-P13-00013\&segmentID=4) and listened to the harrowing stories of communities on the front lines of this crisis [in places like Louisiana’s Cancer Alley.](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=26-P13-00010\&segmentID=1) Worst of all, I knew that the [fossil fuel industry is doubling down on plastic production](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/01/nx-s1-5763690/the-oil-industry-is-betting-big-on-plastics-heres-what-that-means-for-the-futurehttps:/www.npr.org/2026/04/01/nx-s1-5763690/the-oil-industry-is-betting-big-on-plastics-heres-what-that-means-for-the-future) to keep profiting from petrochemicals as we shift toward electrified transportation and clean energy. 

 

And Enck doesn’t shy away from these realities. But, rather than dwelling on the decisions that brought us here, her book focuses on what comes next. 

 

In [this week’s interview](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=26-P13-00029\&segmentID=3) with Host Paloma Beltran, Enck highlights the policy changes, grassroots organizing, and public awareness campaigns that are creating momentum for a plastic-free future. The nonprofit she founded, [Beyond Plastics,](https://www.beyondplastics.org/) is largely successful due to its network of local, grassroots groups – a testament to the power of ordinary people and communities making meaningful progress.

 

And many of those leaders are women. 

 

One of the advocates Judith Enck highlights in her book is Sharon Lavigne, who has become a leading voice in the fight against plastics manufacturers in Cancer Alley. As the founder of [RISE St. James](https://risestjames.org/), Lavigne combines her relentless advocacy and unwavering faith with a commitment to preserving the heritage and culture of the place she calls home. You’ve likely heard her name before, since Lavigne has joined our show in both [2021](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=21-P13-00037\&segmentID=1) and [2022](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=22-P13-00038\&segmentID=1)!

 

Diane Wilson is another anti-plastics crusader featured in *The Problem With Plastic*. She’s a fourth-generation shrimper who lives along the Texas Gulf Coast, and in 2019, she won a historic $50 million settlement against Formosa Plastics for illegal dumping of toxic plastic waste into the waters where she and generations of her family have fished. (Wilson is also a veteran LOE guest, who appeared on our show in [1996,](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=96-P13-00024\&segmentID=7) [1998,](https://loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=98-P13-00023\&segmentID=5) [2005](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=05-P13-00041\&segmentID=5), and [2023!](https://loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=23-P13-00019\&segmentID=2))

 

I’m not surprised that many of the actions against plastics are female-led. It seems to be an increasing trend that women are becoming the leading voices in grassroots environmental movements around the world. This year, for the first time ever, all six of [the Goldman Environmental Prize](https://www.goldmanprize.org/) recipients were women (check out our latest coverage [here!](https://www.loe.org/shows/segments.html?programID=26-P13-00026\&segmentID=2)). And the modern environmental movement was largely catalyzed by Rachel Carson, the marine biologist whose groundbreaking book [*Silent Spring*](https://bookshop.org/p/books/silent-spring-rachel-carson/b797631377260b2d?ean=9780618249060\&bkshp-astro=t) exposed the dangers of synthetic pesticides and directly inspired the creation of the EPA. I remember reading her book in “Introduction to Environmental Studies” my freshman year of college and feeling truly in awe of her legacy. As a young woman trying to envision my role within the environmental field, it was inspiring to see a successful, outspoken female leader spearheading awareness.

 

I’m taking inspiration from these remarkable women – their courage, persistence, and unwavering commitment to their communities – but also their concrete successes in putting the pressure on corporations and the government to take responsibility for their actions. 

 

*The Problem with Plastic* is ultimately a hopeful story. For every large corporation fighting to preserve the status quo, there seems to be countless people like Judith Enck working to build something better.

**Bella Smith**
