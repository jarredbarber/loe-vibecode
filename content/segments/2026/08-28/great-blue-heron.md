LENDER: The fog is like snow. Snow when it is dry and fine and pure white, or blue white. Safe. A blanket. Safe and soft. Like pressing your face into fresh powder, eyes open. And though the sun is awake now, like snow on a very cold morning the fog, remains. And does not burn away. How it glows. The bright wick of a lantern that persists insensitive to the flame. Sound also is made indefinite. Vague. Yet I know what I hear: The roiling, crackling clatter of young great blue herons begging to be fed. I see their shadows now, through the fog. Their mother also, who has been long and far way, down the great river, into the tributaries and the shallows where she steps, carefully, creature of stealth and purpose who seeks until she finds. Only then does she return. She stands now at the rim of the nest high in the bare, bone-like limbs of the tree. Her neck long. Her long beak pointing. As if withholding.

![A mother heron comes to feed her chicks, who stab at her impatiently. (Photo: Mark Seth Lender)](https://loe.org/content/2026-08-28/MSL_motherandchicks.JPG)

Her children reaching, grabbing, stabbing, finally, finally she raises the fish from her crop. And lets it fall, between them…

It’s as if they have never been fed. Never. Neither one. As if the idea of food is novelle. How they struggle, with this pudgy little fish, how to approach it, grasp it, how to take it apart and swallow it, this new object that means, *Life.*

DOERING: That’s Living on Earth explorer in residence, Mark Seth Lender – and you can find pictures at our website, loe dot org.
